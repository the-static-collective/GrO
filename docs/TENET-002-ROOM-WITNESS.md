# TENET 002 — Spatial witness brief

**Gate:** Function concept only  
**Family:** gameplay room  
**Runtime claim:** none  
**Paid asset generation:** none

## Purpose

Make the cross-locality authority boundary readable without explanatory UI.

The player should be able to infer, from architecture alone:

1. transport can arrive without creating a route;
2. local admission may create a route;
3. refusal leaves no fake route;
4. the same incoming particular can have different local consequences.

## Three-space specimen

### Source Garden — Room A

Hero function: author a bounded future possibility.

Carrier architecture: a **mail slot / post aperture**, deliberately non-traversable.

It must not resemble a person-sized portal. Its visual promise is only: *something can be carried from here.*

### Rain Room — Room B

Arrival surface: matching carrier aperture.

Initial state: no traversable doorway attributable to the incoming tenet.

After local ADMIT: a genuine doorway becomes available, with visible threshold, wall thickness, passage depth, and destination space containing the community rain bowl.

The doorway is a consequence of Room B's local admission, not a transported source-room door.

### Quiet Room — Room C

Arrival surface: matching carrier aperture.

After local REFUSE: wall remains architecturally closed.

A receipt object may remain inspectable, but no arch, door surround, or portal silhouette may imply traversability.

## Hero hierarchy

Hero is the **difference between B and C**, not the carrier object itself.

B: readable real aperture + destination.  
C: readable closed wall + receipt witness.

## Navigation

- Primary arrival remains a normal open hallway at least 2.4 m clear width.
- The tenet carrier is not a navigation route.
- Room B's admitted doorway must participate in navigation/collision only after admission state.
- Room C must never expose collision/nav data suggesting the refused route exists.

## Performance budget

Prototype target only:
- procedural shell;
- no generated hero meshes required;
- simple material separation;
- one carrier fixture reused across rooms;
- one rain-bowl prop may be primitive geometry for Function testing.
